About This Manual

MacPaint is for painting pictures on your Macintosh, with tools much like a painter’s own: a paintbrush, a pencil, spraypaint, an eraser, and a paint can that fills whole areas. This manual shows you how.

What you need

To use MacPaint, you need:

  • a Macintosh Plus with System 6.0.7;
  • a hard disk, or 800K floppy disks, for MacPaint and your pictures;
  • an ImageWriter printer, if you’d like to print.

What you should know

This manual assumes you know the basics of your Macintosh: using the mouse, choosing from menus, and working with windows and disks. If you don’t, start with your Macintosh owner’s guide.

How to use this manual

There are three ways in:

  • Learn it: Chapter 1 paints one picture with you, in thirteen short lessons.
  • Look it up: Chapter 2 shows how to do each task, Chapter 3 describes every tool and menu, and the appendixes explain MacPaint’s messages.
  • Just try it: start MacPaint and paint. Undo takes back the last thing you did.

MacPaint 1.6

This manual describes MacPaint 1.6. Nearly all of it applies to MacPaint 1.5 too: Appendix E lists the differences.

Conventions

A step tells you what to do.

The plain text after it tells you what happens.

Grey text adds a remark you can skip.

Keys are named as they’re marked: Return, Backspace, and the Command key, ⌘.

Chapter 1

Learning MacPaint

3The MacPaint Screen

When MacPaint starts, it opens a new, untitled document, with the paintbrush chosen. Everything you paint with is on the screen at once.

The tool palette on the left holds MacPaint’s twenty tools. To choose a tool, click it: the tool you’ve chosen is highlighted.

Below it, the line widths set how thick lines and the borders of shapes are drawn. A check mark shows the width in use.

Along the bottom, the pattern palette holds 38 patterns. The box at its left shows the current pattern: the one you’re painting with.

The drawing window shows part of your picture: 416 dots across and 240 down, out of a page of 576 by 720 dots, 8 by 10 inches. It has no scroll bars. Lesson 10 shows how to see the rest of the page.

For a reminder of the tools and the short cuts, choose Introduction or Short Cuts from the Goodies menu.

10Looking at the Whole Page

The window shows only part of the page. To see the whole page at once:

Choose Show Page from the Goodies menu.

The whole page appears, reduced, with your picture on it. The dotted rectangle marks the part of the page the window shows.

Drag the dotted rectangle to another part of the page, then click OK.

The window now shows that part of the page.

To move your view a little way, use the grabber:

Click the grabber (the hand) in the tool palette, then drag in the window.

The picture moves with the pointer, so you can see another part of the page.

The Menus

The Edit Menu

The Edit menu’s commands change the part of the picture you’ve selected. Until you select something, only Undo and Paste can be chosen.

Undo ⌘Z

Takes back the last change you made to the picture. The ` key does the same.

Cut ⌘X

Removes the selection and puts it on the Clipboard.

Copy ⌘C

Puts a copy of the selection on the Clipboard.

Paste ⌘V

Puts the Clipboard’s picture in the middle of the window, selected, ready to drag into place.

Clear

Erases the selection, leaving white. Backspace does the same.

Invert

Turns the selection’s black dots white and its white dots black.CHECK

Fill

Fills the selection with the current pattern.CHECK

Trace Edges ⌘E

Outlines the shapes in the selection.CHECK

Flip Horizontal

Flips the selection from left to right.CHECK

Flip Vertical

Flips the selection upside down.CHECK

Rotate

Turns the selection a quarter turn anticlockwise.

Flip Horizontal, Flip Vertical and Rotate work on a selection made with the selection rectangle, not the lasso.CHECK
